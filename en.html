01 THE BIG PICTURE

A merger takes a day.
Integration is a long flight.

Bringing Korean Air and Asiana Airlines together is not simply a matter of placing two fleets under one name. Reservations, ticketing, operations control, maintenance, safety manuals, crew training, airport flows, lounges, mileage and corporate culture all have to be rewritten into a single operating language.

In December 2024, Korean Air acquired a 63.88% stake in Asiana Airlines, making the companies parent and subsidiary. The merger agreement was approved in May 2026, followed by conditional government approval in June and approval at Asiana’s extraordinary shareholders’ meeting in August. If the schedule holds, Asiana’s corporate entity and brand will be absorbed into Korean Air on 17 December.

Yet one date on a calendar does not mean every change happens at once. Legal signatures come first; aircraft repainting takes years. Alliance membership can change overnight, while mileage programs can require a decade-long transition. This webzine places those different clocks on one map.

02 SIX YEARS IN MOTION

Six years,
fourteen jurisdictions,
one conclusion.

Between the acquisition announcement and the planned launch of the integrated airline came remedies from competition authorities around the world, a cargo-business sale, replacement carriers on overlapping routes and changes to operational certification.

  1. 2020.11
    Acquisition announced

    Korean Air resolved to acquire Asiana Airlines and signed a share subscription agreement, beginning a major restructuring of Korea’s aviation sector during the pandemic.

  2. 2021.01
    Competition filings begin

    Reviews started in Korea and other major jurisdictions, with authorities examining reduced competition in both passenger and cargo markets.

  3. 2022.02
    KFTC grants conditional approval

    Structural and behavioral remedies were imposed, including slot and traffic-right transfers and monitoring of fares, capacity and service.

  4. 2023.03
    UK approval

    Remedies were accepted to support Virgin Atlantic’s entry on the London–Seoul route. Slot arrangements were further formalized in 2026.

  5. 2024.01–02
    Japan and EU approvals

    Japan required remedies on selected passenger and cargo routes; the EU required replacement competition on four European passenger routes and divestiture of Asiana’s cargo business.

  6. 2024.12
    Stake acquisition completed

    After the U.S. process concluded, Korean Air acquired 63.88% of Asiana. Asiana continued operating independently as a subsidiary during the transition period.

  7. 2025.08
    Cargo business divested

    Asiana’s cargo operation and 11 freighters were transferred to Air Incheon, now Air Zeta, implementing a key condition of the EU and Japan approvals.

  8. 2026.01
    Shared Incheon T2 hub

    All Asiana flights moved to Terminal 2, delivering one of the first visible signs of physical integration for passengers.

  9. 2026.05–08
    Merger contract, approval and vote

    The merger contract was approved in May, conditionally authorized by the transport ministry in June and approved by Asiana shareholders in August.

  10. 2026.12.17
    Integrated Korean Air planned to launch

    Asiana is scheduled to leave Star Alliance at 23:59 the previous day and move into a single-company, SkyTeam-centered structure under Korean Air the following day.

03 IDENTITY, REWRITTEN

ONE

More than sharing a logo,
it is about keeping the same promise.

VISUAL

A new face after 41 years

In 2025, Korean Air unveiled a simplified taegeuk motif, a deep-navy wordmark and a metallic sky-blue fuselage. The identity will become the face of the integrated airline, while repainting the full fleet will unfold gradually over several years.

SERVICE

A brand is the sum of every touchpoint

New menus, premium economy, expanded lounges at Incheon T2 and revised check-in flows are changes passengers can feel before a logo changes. The success of the integrated brand will depend on reducing inconsistencies from the booking screen to baggage claim.

MEMORY

After the name Asiana

Since its 1988 launch, Asiana built a generation of travel memories through its multicolor wing, service language and Star Alliance network. The end of a brand should be treated not as deletion, but as an archive project asking what deserves to be preserved and carried into the new standard.

04 NETWORK & FLEET

A larger network,
with less duplication.

Network

From spring 2026, the airlines began codesharing on selected domestic and international routes, including Paris, Sydney and Barcelona. After integration there is more room to redistribute overlapping timings and strengthen connections, while replacement carriers on regulated routes act as a competitive safeguard.

Fleet

A large fleet made up of different manufacturers, subtypes and cabin layouts cannot be standardized overnight. Orders for more than 100 next-generation Boeing aircraft point to the long-term direction, but the first integration work will be felt in passenger-facing standards such as seat numbering and service procedures.

LCC

Jin Air, Air Busan and Air Seoul are expected to be reorganized into a separate integrated Jin Air, currently targeted for the first quarter of 2027. Scale and a broader regional-airport network come with new questions about Busan identity, brand diversity and competition.

06 PEOPLE BEHIND THE MERGER
“An operating certificate can be unified.
Trust can only be built through time spent working together.”

The companies have increased points of contact through joint emergency-evacuation drills, shared offices, community programs and family events. Reports on an internal survey in late 2025 said 57.4% of respondents viewed the integration positively. Yet differences in rank, pay, rostering, promotion and service culture cannot be solved by numbers alone. The “one airline” passengers encounter will ultimately be built on fairness employees can actually feel.

07 BEFORE THE NAME DISAPPEARS
1988—2026

Before the name
Asiana disappears.

A brand does not truly end when its logo comes down, but when people stop booking journeys under its name. Across 38 years, the multicolor wing accompanied first overseas trips, study abroad, business travel, homecomings and long nights of connection.

Integration naturally pursues efficiency, but memory is not an efficiency exercise. The cadence of a safety announcement, the color of a crew scarf, a meal tray, a paper boarding pass or an alliance lounge sign can preserve the texture of an era. What the new airline must keep is not necessarily the old name, but the way trust was built beneath it.

08 THE GUARDRAILS

Another word
for approval: conditions.

A major-airline combination can increase connectivity and scale, but it can also remove competitors. That is why approvals around the world came not as a simple yes, but as bundles of route-specific obligations.

EU

Four European routes + cargo

Assets had to be made available so replacement carriers could enter Paris, Frankfurt, Rome and Barcelona, while Asiana’s freighter business had to be sold to a qualified buyer.

UK

London–Seoul slots

A commitment to provide Heathrow slots for Virgin Atlantic’s London–Seoul entry was accepted, with the UK CMA approving a more detailed slot agreement in March 2026.

JAPAN

Competition on Japan routes

The transaction was approved subject to slot transfers on selected Korea–Japan passenger routes and cargo remedies. Completion of the cargo divestiture was a core part of implementation.

KOREA

Monitoring 34 overlapping routes

Measures included transfers of slots and traffic rights plus temporary monitoring of fares, capacity and service quality. Replacement carriers were selected on routes including Seattle, Honolulu, Jakarta and Jeju.

Regulatory remedies can be implemented or amended over time. For travel decisions, always prioritize the latest notices from the relevant authorities and airlines.

10 QUICK ANSWERS

Questions
still in the air.

When exactly does Asiana Airlines disappear?

The current official schedule targets 17 December 2026 for the launch of the integrated Korean Air. Asiana is scheduled to leave Star Alliance at 23:59 on 16 December, although aircraft repainting and some system changes may continue in phases afterward.

What happens to an Asiana ticket I already bought?

It does not mean the flight simply disappears. Around the merger, flight number, operating carrier, seat and terminal may be adjusted, so check your booking and airline emails. Partner award tickets may have additional restrictions around the alliance change.

Do Asiana miles disappear automatically?

Under the publicly disclosed proposal, no. Legacy Asiana miles would be held separately for 10 years after integration, then remaining balances would convert automatically to SKYPASS at the end of the period. Original expiration dates remain, subject to the final approved plan.

How long do Star Alliance benefits last?

According to Asiana’s official notice, Star Alliance earning, redemption and elite benefits continue through 16 December 2026. Deadlines for retroactive credit or award tickets may be earlier, so check the official timetable.

Will ticket prices rise?

No one can state that with certainty. Integration can improve connectivity and efficiency, but it also reduces direct competition on overlapping routes. That is why regulators imposed slot transfers, replacement-carrier entry and fare/service monitoring.

Is this an official Korean Air website?

No. It is an independent editorial webzine based on publicly available information. For tickets, mileage, operations or investment decisions, always prioritize the latest official notices from Korean Air, Asiana Airlines and the relevant authorities.
